Lab 01 — Packet Tracer

SOHO Wireless Network

End-to-end setup of a small office / home office network: physical cabling, wireless router administration through its web GUI, a tightened DHCP pool, WPA2-Personal security and host connectivity testing.

What I configured

  • Cabled the physical layer: coaxial feed into a cable splitter, split to the cable modem and TV, then copper straight-through to the router.
  • Administered the SOHO wireless router through its web GUI and replaced the default administrative credentials with a custom password policy.
  • Configured the DHCP pool with a 10-lease cap to reduce lease exhaustion and limit unknown devices attaching to the network.
  • Enabled the 2.4 GHz radio with a custom SSID and WPA2-Personal (AES), then authenticated and associated a wireless laptop.

Verification

  • Confirmed both wired hosts picked up valid IPv4 addresses in the 192.168.x.x scope over DHCP.
  • Authenticated the laptop against the SSID with the pre-shared key and checked access point association.
  • Loaded an external domain from every endpoint and verified DNS resolution, routing through the cable gateway and access on both wired and wireless segments.

Lab 02 — Packet Tracer

DHCP on a Wireless Router

Router management and DHCP scope design: connecting client workstations, re-addressing the router, resizing the DHCP scope and proving every lease with command-line checks.

What I configured

  • Deployed three client PCs and a router, cabled from the PC FastEthernet interfaces to the router switch ports with copper straight-through cable.
  • Set the clients to request IP settings dynamically, then identified the default gateway handed out by the router.
  • Re-addressed the router from its factory default to 192.168.5.1 and re-authenticated to the management page on the new IP.
  • Rebuilt the DHCP scope: starting address 192.168.5.126 with a maximum of 75 users.

Verification

  • Renewed leases by toggling clients between static and DHCP, then confirmed the assignment with ipconfig.
  • Rolled dynamic addressing out to the remaining workstations and confirmed each one pulled an address from the new scope.
  • Pinged the router management interface and peer workstations to prove internal reachability.
